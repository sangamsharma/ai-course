## Your First Real Win
### Follow along and finish with something you'll actually use — takes about 12 minutes

⏱ **12 minutes**

Reading about AI is one thing. Using it to solve a real problem you have *right now* is where it clicks. In this lesson, we're going to do one complete task together — from a rough idea to a finished result you can actually send. By the end, you'll have made something useful.

> 📌 **Do this now — don't just read**
> Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini in another tab or window right now — whichever one you picked in the last lesson. This lesson only works if you do it alongside the AI tool, not after. Reading without doing = forgetting. Doing = learning.

We're going to write a short, friendly message — something like inviting friends to a get-together, or any short message you actually need to send this week. We'll do it in four quick rounds, each one making the result better.

**Round 1 — The Basic Ask** *(30 seconds)*
- Open your AI tool and type something simple, like: *"Write an email inviting friends to a barbecue this Saturday at 3 PM at my place."*
- Read what comes back. It's... fine? But kind of generic. It doesn't sound like *you*.
- Notice: the AI guessed the tone, the length, and the details, because you didn't give it much to work with. It did its best with very little information.

**Round 2 — Add Your Voice** *(30 seconds)*
- Now add more detail. Try: *"Write a warm, casual email inviting close friends to a backyard barbecue this Saturday at 3 PM. It's a potluck — I'll provide burgers and drinks, guests can bring sides or desserts. Kids are welcome, and there'll be lawn games. My name is [Your Name]."*
- Compare this to Round 1. More personal? More useful?
- Every detail you add — the event type, the vibe, what to bring, who's invited — makes the result more usable.

**Round 3 — Fix What You Don't Like** *(30 seconds)*
- Read Round 2's result again. Does any sentence sound too stiff? A phrase you'd never actually say? Tell the AI: *"Make it more casual — like I'm texting a friend, not writing a formal invitation. Remove any corporate-sounding language."*
- This is the key skill: **you don't have to accept the first response.** You can ask for changes to anything — tone, length, formality, details.
- The AI won't get offended if you ask for changes. Keep going until it sounds right.

**Round 4 — Polish and Use** *(30 seconds)*
- Final check: read the message out loud. Does it sound like something *you'd* actually say? If yes, you're done. If not, ask for one more tweak: *"Make the opening line more exciting"* or *"Add a line reminding people to reply by Friday."*
- Copy the final version. You just created something personal and useful in about two minutes — faster, and better, than starting from a blank page.

> 💡 **The 4-Round Method — use this for everything, not just today**
> Round 1: Give the basic task. Round 2: Add context, detail, and your voice. Round 3: Fix what you don't like. Round 4: Final polish.
> Most people stop at Round 1 and wonder why AI sounds generic. The real result happens in Rounds 2–4.

### 🖊️ Try it yourself: Apply the 4-Round Method to your own task

**Instructions:** Think of something real you need to write this week — not a made-up example. It could be a message, an email, a short note, or a list. Work through all 4 rounds with your own task, then write your Round 4 version here.

**Starter prompt (copy and edit):**
```
I need to [describe your real task]. Context: [who it's for, any important
details]. My style is [casual / professional / warm / direct]. Make sure it
[specific thing you want].
```

**Make it your own (fill in the blanks):**
```
I need to ____________________. Context: ____________________.
My style is ____________________. Make sure it ____________________.
```

**What good output looks like:** You read your final version out loud and it sounds like *you* wrote it — not like a template. It mentions at least one specific, personal detail (a name, a date, an actual reason) that a generic version wouldn't have. If you'd genuinely send or use this today, you've succeeded.

**Common mistakes and how to fix them:**
- *Stopping after Round 1 because "it's good enough."* Fix: always do at least Round 3 — asking for one specific change — before deciding it's done.
- *Giving vague feedback like "make it better."* Fix: name the actual thing you want changed — "more casual," "shorter," "remove the second paragraph" — the same way Round 3 above does.
- *Forgetting to personalize before sending.* Fix: swap in real names, dates, or details the AI couldn't have known, even after a great Round 4.

**Level-up challenge:** Once you've got a result you're happy with, paste the exact same Round 1 prompt into a *different* AI tool (if you tried ChatGPT, try Claude or Gemini) and compare. Which one needed fewer rounds to get somewhere good?

### Key takeaways
- The 4-Round Method: Basic → Add Context → Fix Tone → Polish.
- Always iterate — never accept the first draft.
- Tell AI exactly what to change ("make it more casual," "remove that line") — vague feedback gets vague results.
- If it doesn't sound like you when read aloud, it's not done yet.
